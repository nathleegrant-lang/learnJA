Spot the maths in the street

Lines and angles are everywhere in Jamaica: on our roads, our rooftops and even on the Half Way Tree clock. Can you find the hidden maths?

🎯 What you will learn today

  • describe points, lines, line segments and rays
  • identify parallel, intersecting and perpendicular lines
  • name angles as acute, right, obtuse, straight or reflex
  • use angle facts to find missing angles
  • find equal and supplementary angles made by a transversal crossing parallel lines
Curriculum objectives (NSC Mathematics, Grade 8 | Term 1 | Unit 4: Geometry)

Strand: Geometry

Standard: Explore paths, geometric shapes, and space and make generalizations about geometric relationships within the environment.

Specific objectives. Students will be able to:

  • Investigate the relationship among angles formed by: (a) a transversal and two or more parallel lines; (b) intersecting non-perpendicular lines.

Also reviewed: points, lines, segments and rays; parallel and perpendicular lines; and naming angles by size. These are prior learning for this objective.

Source: National Standards Curriculum Guide, Mathematics Grades 7-9 (MoEYI, 2018), Grade 8, Term 1, Unit 4 (Geometry). This lesson introduces it early, in Grade 7.

    Types of angles

    We name angles by their size. Turn the hand anticlockwise from the start line and watch the name change.

    Your mission

    1. Turn the hand to make each of the 5 angle types below.
    2. Right and straight angles must be exact, so use −1 and +1 to fine-tune.
    3. Collect all 5 for a star.
    120°
    Acute: more than 0° and less than 90°.
    Right: exactly 90°.
    Obtuse: more than 90° and less than 180°.
    Straight: exactly 180°.
    Reflex: more than 180° and less than 360°.
    Full turn: exactly 360°.

    Inna Patois: Less than 90 a acute. Exactly 90 a right angle. Between 90 an 180 a obtuse. More than 180 a reflex.

    UDL in this lesson

    Engagement: local Jamaican contexts, a prediction to test, choice of challenge level, stars, and self-assessment. Representation: every idea appears as a picture, a moving model, and text; read-aloud, text size, easy-read font, high contrast, a word bank and optional Patois support. Action and expression: tap, drag or keyboard; −1/+1 buttons for fine control; answers by choosing, typing or sorting; written reflection and a printable summary.

    Common misconceptions to watch for

    • Thinking a longer arm makes a bigger angle (the door model addresses this: the angle is the turn).
    • Thinking parallel lines must be horizontal.
    • Mixing up co-interior (add to 180°) with alternate (equal).
    • Reading the wrong scale on a protractor; follow up with a hands-on protractor activity.
    • Believing the angle rules work when lines are not parallel.

    Extension ideas

    Photograph angles around the school compound and classify them; measure real angles with a protractor; design a road junction plan with parallel streets and a transversal.
